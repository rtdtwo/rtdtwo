# Name Help

This is a guide to pronouncing my name properly. It is also a way for me to flex my understanding of [Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages) and [Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_languages) phonology.

English (Romanized): Rishabh Tatiraju

Hindi (Devanagari): ऋषभ तातिराजु

Telugu: రిషబ్ తాతిరాజు

## How to pronounce my name?

`Rishabh` is my first name. It's a _Sanskrit_ name. `Rishab` is a widely used alternative, but I prefer the original.
- `Rish-`, like `Rich` or `Rick` but with the `sh` (like in `shoe`) at the end.
- `-abh`, `ab` (like in `abuse`).

> **Why do some people drop the final "h"?** In `Rishabh`, `bh` represents one consonant - an aspirated `b`, not a `b` followed by a separate `h`. The aspiration can be very subtle, so the ending may sound like `b` to some listeners. In Hindi, the final inherent vowel is also usually dropped. I prefer a light breathy release on the final consonant, but don't add a full extra "h" sound.

> The Sanskrit word *ṛṣabha* means [bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull). In [Indian classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_music), [Rishabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishabha_(svara)) is the name of the second swara - a musical note, roughly like *Re* in *Do Re Mi Fa So La Ti*. The note is traditionally associated with a bull's lowing. My family has musical roots, so I was named after the note.

`Tatiraju` is my last name:
- `Tati-` is close to `Tatty`, but both `T`s are **dental T sounds**: your tongue touches your upper teeth rather than the ridge just behind them. English doesn't have this as a distinct `T` sound, so `Tatty` is close enough!
- `-raju` sounds like `Ra` (the Egyptian sun god) + `ju` (as in `juice`).

> Tatiraju is a rare [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_people) last name; in my ancestral research, most bearers I've documented share a common lineage.

## What's in a name?
A lot, actually - especially with Indian names having beautiful meanings turning into something opposite when non-Native speakers pronounce them _(see [Hardik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardik))_. So, thank you for showing interest in pronouncing my name the correct way.

Many Indian names have roots in [_Sanskrit_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit), a classical language whose earliest works date to around 1500 BCE. Sanskrit has had a long history as a language of literature, religion, and scholarship - you can think of it as the *Latin* of India. My native language, [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_people), is heavily influenced by Sanskrit, especially in its vocabulary and literary tradition. But Telugu has a different lineage: it belongs to the Dravidian language family, not the Indo-Aryan family that Sanskrit belongs to.

_Sanskrit_ can be challenging to master, even for many Indians, and it is no longer widely spoken as an everyday first language. It remains culturally significant, though: the _[Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas)_ were preserved through generations of [oral recitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas#Transmission), while _Sanskrit_ texts have also been transmitted in writing. _Sanskrit_'s legacy lives on in many modern Indian languages. My native language, Telugu, is Dravidian in origin but has been deeply influenced by Sanskrit.
